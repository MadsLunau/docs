There are two ways to identify gaps:

- **Reactive:** Prompt the Internal Agent for a report on how your Customer Agent is performing and where it falls short.
- **Proactive:** Set up automations that watch your tools and your support queue and surface gaps on their own.

Either way, the Internal Agent reports the impact and proposes the update as a todo in a [task](/platform/tasks). Each todo starts the next build, and the loop keeps turning.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Gaps surface">
    Reactively, when you prompt the Internal Agent for a report. Proactively, when automations watching GitHub, Linear, Slack, and your support queue catch a product change or a human-handled ticket - or when the Customer Agent flags missing context or a tool connection on a ticket (in beta).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The Internal Agent proposes updates">
    It creates todos for the high-leverage improvements it identifies in automations and one-off tasks: knowledge gaps, tool ideas, and flow improvements. Each todo comes with the relevant context and attributes, assigned to the right user or team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You start the todo">
    One click opens the task, and the next build begins. See [Build](/internal-agent/build).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Todos appear on the [Tasks page](/platform/tasks) and in your [Inbox](/platform/inbox).

## Reactive: prompt for a report

Prompt the Internal Agent in a task whenever you want to know how your Customer Agent is doing. It covers both the numbers and the why - including the impact of every release on your resolution rate.

- **Quantitative reports.** _"Show me a graph of resolved tickets over the past month"_ gets you an interactive chart, sortable by category. It can analyze any slice of your ticket data.
- **Qualitative reports.** Patterns, trends, and representative quotes across your tickets. Common ones: performance reports, bug-pattern reports, and improvement reports.

  ![A Customer Agent Performance report for the last 30 days, generated by the Internal Agent](/snippets/reporting.png)
</Frame>

When a report reveals a gap - a ticket type the agent keeps transferring, a policy it gets wrong - the Internal Agent proposes the fix as a todo in the same task.

<Accordion title="How tickets are counted" icon="sitemap">
  Every ticket falls into one branch:

  - **Engaged:** Your Customer Agent replied to the ticket or took action on it.
    - **Resolved:** The agent closed the ticket, and it stayed closed for at least 3 days. Split into general, spam, and merged tickets.
    - **Engaged, not resolved:** The agent engaged, then handed the ticket to a human for final resolution (previously called "assisted").
  - **Not engaged:** Your Customer Agent never worked the ticket.
    - **Not assigned:** The ticket was never assigned to the agent, for example because your routing workflow excluded it.
    - **Ignored:** The ticket was assigned to the agent, but it stayed out, for example because the ticket type is out of scope in its instructions.
</Accordion>

Your Customer Agent also works as a team member in your ticketing system, so your existing reports track it automatically: SLAs, CSAT surveys, agent-level dashboards. We can also share report templates with the key metrics we recommend for every supported system.

## Proactive: automations

An automation is an Internal Agent task that runs on a schedule or a trigger (see [Automations](/platform/automations)). Set it up once, and it keeps watching for gaps - no prompt needed:

- **Your support queue:** learn from tickets a human handled, and catch the questions your Customer Agent should have answered.
- **Your tools:** a PR merging in GitHub, an issue moving to Done in Linear, a page changing in Notion - anything that means your Customer Agent needs an update.
- **Recurring reports:** the same quantitative and qualitative reports, daily or weekly, with the gaps they reveal turned into todos.

When an automation finds a high-leverage improvement, the Internal Agent creates a todo with the relevant context and attributes, assigned to the right user or team.

The Customer Agent flags gaps too: when it's missing context or a tool connection on a ticket, it kicks off the Internal Agent to investigate (in beta).
